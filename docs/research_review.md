## DELIVERABLE 1: executive research diagnosis

1. **The decisive weakness is the data, not the algorithm.** [F] In the dataset, the slicing/scheduler configuration is fixed for the whole reservation (Paper p. 5: "none of the estimators observes a mid-episode action switch"; ColO-RAN maintainers describe the same "static policy" property for their Colosseum datasets). [S] Logs with one constant action per episode cannot identify how the system evolves *after a decision changes*. This is exactly what Reviewer 2 (static benchmark) and, indirectly, Reviewer 1 (Markov property) object to. Lu reaches the same conclusion independently: "A static set of KPMs is not enough to reconstruct a sequential decision problem… useful for system identification, offline model calibration, or traffic generation, but insufficient as a complete DRL benchmark unless they are paired with decision logs" (Lu §X-C, p. 48).
2. **The safety claim is a CMDP constraint, held in expectation and measured with proxies; it is not a guarantee.** [S] Safe-CQL puts the constraint into the optimisation (Lagrangian, adaptive λ, Paper Eq. 11–12). However: the cost model is myopic (γ_c = 0) and tabular over load bins; the budget is met only on average (tabular OPE cost 0.030 ± 0.001 sits exactly at d = 0.03); and deployment-time safety (shield, fallback, online check) is not addressed. Lu states that Lagrangian methods guarantee J_C ≤ d "only in expectation and strictly upon convergence" (Lu §II-H, p. 11). Fig. 3 of the paper shows λ still rising and the training cost still ≈ 0.032 > d at epoch 100, which contradicts the text's "converges near 12.9".
3. **A trivial baseline may match the agent; this must be checked first.** [H] In Fig. 1 of the paper, the static configuration (39 eMBB PRBs, RR) has a violation rate of 0.00 / 0.02 / 0.03 / 0.03 across the four load levels, so it is ≤ d in every bin. eMBB throughput increases with eMBB PRBs (Pearson +0.73, p. 3), and RR "match[es] or exceed[s] PF at every split" (p. 3–4). The fixed rule "always (39, RR)" is therefore plausibly feasible and near-optimal under the paper's own tabular model. If it matches Safe-CQL, the RL contribution disappears. This is the first experiment to run (≈ 1 day).
4. **Positioning errors are cheap to fix but must be fixed.** [F] With a 5 s decision period, the loop falls in the Non-RT RIC range (> 1 s), not the Near-RT range (10 ms – 1 s) (Lu §III, p. 14). [F] The "URLLC" slice uses L_max = 20 ms and tolerates a 3 % violation budget (97 %), whereas URLLC targets are 1 ms at 1 − 10⁻⁵ (Haque p. 1–2). [F] The testbed is LTE, 10 MHz / 50 PRBs, 8 static UEs (dataset README). [H] The paper should present itself as configuration-level control of a latency-sensitive slice by an rApp or slow xApp. It should not claim URLLC or Near-RT xApp control unless redesigned.
5. **The most defensible thesis direction is constrained offline-to-online learning on decision-logged data that I generate myself.** [S] Lu names "transfer learning with conservative offline RL… then shadow mode or constrained online fine-tuning" as its first research direction (Lu §XII-A, p. 54). The paper's own pipeline is the natural offline half of that direction. [H] A calibrated ns-3 5G-LENA environment with logged, randomised behaviour policies (known propensities, action switches) removes the identification problem. It enables counterfactual evaluation and supports a sub-second control loop. The commercial-traffic twinning dataset then becomes a *traffic and calibration* source, not the training ground truth.

## DELIVERABLE 2: three-survey comparison

### 2.1 Per-survey findings that matter for my research

**Haque et al. (IEEE COMST 2025): what must be guaranteed**

- [F] URLLC targets: 99.999 % reliability at up to 1 ms (p. 1). Reliability requirements range from 1 − 10⁻⁵ to 1 − 10⁻⁹ (p. 2). Reliability is defined (NGMN) as packets delivered within the deadline (deck slide 7, p. 2–3).
- [F] Coexistence with eMBB is named as a core URLLC design challenge, alongside control overhead and resource optimisation. The authors call for joint queue/delay/error models (§V, p. 9; deck slide 9).
- [F] The ML approaches reviewed are supervised, unsupervised (including a primal-dual DNN learning Lagrange multipliers) and RL/FRL (Table IX, p. 25; §IX-C, pp. 26–27). These include an O-RAN federated DRL coordinating two xApps (p. 27).
- [F] 6G directions (§XI, p. 29): sub-ms latency; 99.99999 % reliability; "a thorough analysis of the trade-offs among the latency and reliability is needed"; MAML for fast adaptation to topology change; communication–control co-design.
- [S] Implication: the paper's "URLLC" slice (20 ms, 97 %) is far from these targets. The relevant guarantee is a tail-latency/reliability constraint, not a window-average.

**Lu et al. (arXiv 2026): how a learning agent controls the RAN**

- [F] Offline RL: central risk is distributional shift; BCQ, CQL and IQL are the representative answers. "Practical offline DRL for O-RAN must constrain or regularize actions that the logged dataset does not support" (§II-G, p. 11).
- [F] Constrained RL: CMDP and Lagrangian primal-dual updates. These are guaranteed "only in expectation and strictly upon convergence" (§II-H, p. 11).
- [F] O-RAN timescales: Non-RT RIC > 1 s; Near-RT RIC 10 ms – 1 s; dApps < 10 ms (§III, p. 14). Scheduler reconfiguration is typically near-real-time; slice orchestration is non-real-time (§IV-E, p. 19).
- [F] Training strategies: offline, online (in simulators/testbeds: ns-O-RAN, ColO-RAN, OpenRAN Gym) and hybrid. Behaviour-policy provenance, delay alignment and reward reconstruction are part of the data pipeline. Examples: 2OffRAN (offline + OPE for handover) and SafeSlice (§IX-A, p. 41).
- [F] DRL datasets must be decision-centred: τ = {(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}, Δt, z_t)}. They should record the behaviour policy and the O-RAN control location, and cover stressed conditions (§X-C, Eq. 32, pp. 48–49).
- [F] No widely adopted DRL-for-O-RAN benchmark exists. Results are sensitive to state, action granularity, reward, traffic, control interval, seeds and baseline tuning (§X-D, p. 49). PandORA shows that formulation choices change which agent wins (§IV, p. 16; deck slide 20).
- [F] Future directions (§XII): conservative offline RL + transfer, then shadow mode or constrained fine-tuning, with change detection and rollback (A, p. 54); design-time + runtime assurance, shields, CVaR/tail constraints (B, p. 54); uncertainty-aware twins evaluated on policy-level fidelity (D, p. 55); benchmarks reporting SLA violations, seeds and behaviour-policy provenance (G, p. 56).

**Adhikari et al. (IEEE Access 2024): how the services share resources**

- [F] Coexistence mechanisms: puncturing and superposition, OMA/NOMA/RSMA, slicing, scheduling, flexible TTI (§II–III). Rate-loss models (linear, convex, threshold) determine how URLLC load is charged to eMBB (§II-A, p. 6).
- [F] Learning-based works (§III-D/E, Tables 8–9, pp. 18–20) include Alsenwi et al. (DRL, eMBB rate under URLLC reliability constraint, eMBB reliability > 90 %). These are trained online, in simulation. "Offline" in this survey means offline optimisation with perfect CSI (deck slide 32).
- [F] Open challenges (§V, pp. 25–26): under-utilisation from URLLC reservation; "strong and concrete mathematical models… are still missing" and existing ones are too complex; eMBB loss "almost impossible to nullify… joint optimization… highly recommended"; RIS/UAV add delay; NN/DL "cannot correctly represent the fast changing network situations… results are not universally valid"; DRL improves validity but adds complexity.
- [S] Implication: the paper should state its implicit rate-loss/sharing model (slice-level PRB partition with no puncturing) and report results across URLLC loads.

### 2.3 Cross-survey synthesis [S]

- **Common themes.** (i) The latency–reliability–eMBB-throughput trade-off must be optimised jointly (Haque §V, §XI; Adhikari §V). (ii) Learning-based control is mainstream but almost always trained online in simulation (Haque §IX; Adhikari §III-D; Lu §IX-A). (iii) Constraints should stay explicit instead of being folded into the reward (Lu §II-H, §VIII-A p. 37; Adhikari's constrained works). (iv) Results depend strongly on load and on the formulation (Adhikari flexible-TTI results; Lu PandORA).
- **Complementarity.** Haque fixes *what* must hold (targets, definitions). Adhikari fixes *how* resources are shared (mechanisms, loss models). Lu fixes *who* decides, *when* and *from what data* (RIC placement, timescales, datasets, deployment).
- **A structural tension the surveys expose but do not resolve.** URLLC decisions in Haque and Adhikari happen at TTI/mini-slot scale (≤ 1 ms). Near-RT xApps act at 10 ms – 1 s. So an xApp cannot schedule URLLC packets; it can only *configure* the scheduler and slice quotas that shape URLLC tails. [H] This is the honest, defensible framing for my work: **configuration-level control with tail-latency constraints**, at 100 ms – 1 s (xApp) or > 1 s (rApp).
- **Differences in terminology.** "Offline" means offline optimisation in Adhikari but offline RL in Lu. "URLLC" means 3GPP-grade targets in Haque but a slice label in O-RAN testbeds (including the dataset used in the paper).
- **Underexplored, with caveats.** Within the three surveys, no work learns an eMBB/URLLC slice-configuration policy from logs under an explicit latency constraint. Outside them, there is close work: Yang et al. (2024) apply offline RL (CQL, IQL) to RAN slicing (abstract-level, safety not stated); 2OffRAN (ICMLCN 2025) does offline RL + OPE for handover in O-RAN; CPQ (Xu et al., AAAI 2022) is a safe offline CQL-type algorithm. A systematic search is still needed before claiming any gap.
- **Maturity of experimental approaches.** Abstract environments dominate the coexistence literature. Trace-driven evaluation is rare and limited by missing decision logs (Lu p. 48). Testbed and emulator work (Colosseum, OpenRAN Gym) exists but is costly. Benchmarks are missing (Lu p. 49).

## DELIVERABLE 3: paper and reviewer audit

### 3.1 Technical reconstruction of Offline Safe-CQL

| Element | What the paper does (location) |
|---|---|
| Problem | Jointly choose the eMBB/URLLC PRB split and the MAC scheduler to maximise eMBB throughput under a URLLC latency-violation budget (§I, §III) |
| System | 1 base station, N_PRB = 50, two slices; the xApp is said to sit in the Near-RT RIC (§III-A) |
| Data | WINES Open RAN Commercial Traffic Twinning dataset (Colosseum): real LTE traffic twinned with MGEN; 3 clusters × 5 slicing configurations × 2 schedulers = 30 configurations; 1,116 reservations; up to 8 UEs; 225,313 transitions (§IV-A, p. 3) |
| Decision step | Non-overlapping 5 s windows; ≈ 4 Hz KPMs, ≈ 20 samples per UE per window (§III-C, p. 2) |
| State | s_t ∈ ℝ¹²: UE count, mean RSRP/SNR/MCS/BLER, buffer, ρ_t, ℓ_t + lags (Eq. 2–3). **Excludes the current PRB split and scheduler** (p. 3) |
| Action | 8 discrete actions: {9, 21, 30, 39} eMBB PRBs × {RR, PF} (Eq. 4, Table I); the 50/0 configuration is excluded |
| Reward | Sum over eMBB UEs of the window-mean downlink bitrate, in Mbps (Eq. 5); mean 7.65 Mbps |
| Cost | Per-window fraction of URLLC packets with one-way latency > L_max = 20 ms (Eq. 6); mean 0.053; 33 % of windows contain a violation |
| Problem class | CMDP: max E[Σγᵗr_t] s.t. E[c_t] ≤ d = 0.03, γ = 0.7 (Eq. 7); γ = 0.99 diverges (p. 3) |
| Algorithm | CQL (α = 0.5, double-Q, Polyak) for Q_r; **tabular cost ĉ(a, β)** over load bins with fallback when N < 20 (Eq. 10); policy argmax_a Q_r − λ ĉ (Eq. 11), with γ_c = 0; λ updated by projected ascent after each epoch (Eq. 12) |
| Evaluation | Train on clusters 2–3, test on held-out cluster 1. Matching OPE (≈ 40 % coverage), tabular OPE, FQE (ranking only). BC and CQL: 5 seeds. **Safe-CQL: 2 seeds** (§VI-A) |
| Result | Cost 0.030 vs 0.040–0.057; throughput −1–2 % (Table II, p. 6). "25–47 % fewer violations" |
| Stated novelty | "the algorithm itself is not novel"; the contribution is a trace-grounded formulation, load-aware empirical safety model and rigorous OPE (§II, p. 2) |

**Assumptions needed for the claims to hold [S]:**

- (a) Logged configurations are representative of the deployment cluster. The paper documents confounding of configuration with load (§IV-C).
- (b) The effect of a configuration is confined to the current window: γ_c = 0, "near-immediate effect".
- (c) Switching configuration between windows does not create transient dynamics. This is never observed in the data.
- (d) The load bin is a sufficient statistic for violation risk.
- (e) The 20 ms / 3 % SLA is the relevant service requirement.

### D2: constrained offline→online control (recommended thesis core)

1. **Research question.** Can a policy pre-trained offline on logged slice-configuration decisions be fine-tuned online with bounded cumulative tail-latency violations, under traffic distribution shift, faster than online-from-scratch constrained RL?
2. **Motivation.** Lu §XII-A p. 54 (conservative offline RL → shadow mode → constrained fine-tuning); Lu §II-H p. 11 (Lagrangian unsafe before convergence); Haque §XI p. 29 (latency–reliability trade-off); Adhikari §V p. 26 (joint optimisation).
3. **Relation to the rejected paper.** It reuses the audit, CQL + Lagrangian and the cost model as the offline stage, and fixes the static-data and timescale problems.
4. **Gap to validate in primary literature.** Safe offline-to-online RL in RAN slicing. Check Nagib et al. (JSAC 2023, hybrid transfer learning for safe slicing; online), SafeSlice (ICMLCN 2025; online), Yang et al. 2024 (offline slicing), 2OffRAN (offline handover). In ML, check FISOR (ICLR 2024; offline only) and safe offline-to-online methods (2025, sparse). **Not verified as a gap yet.**
5. **Proposed contribution.** (i) A decision-logged eMBB/latency-sensitive dataset generator with known behaviour propensities. (ii) An offline→online algorithm with a support-aware action filter (shield: only actions with sufficient data support or certified by a cost model upper bound) and a chance/CVaR constraint on per-packet latency. (iii) A measurement of cumulative violations during fine-tuning.
6. **Mathematical changes.**
   - Constraint: P(ℓ > L_max) ≤ ε or CVaR_α(ℓ) ≤ L_max per window, instead of the mean rate.
   - Cost critic: distributional or quantile.
   - Decision period: 100 ms – 1 s.
   - State: include a_{t−1} and switching cost.
   - Online stage: Lagrangian with a pessimistic (upper-confidence) cost estimate; shield = argmax over {a : UCB_c(s, a) ≤ d}.
7. **Data / simulator.** ns-3 + 5G-LENA (NR numerologies, RR/PF/QoS schedulers, BWP- or RB-mask-based slicing), traffic driven by the Colosseum twinning traces; Python agent via ns3-ai/ns3-gym.
8. **Baselines and metrics.** Online PPO-Lag / SAC-Lag from scratch; offline-only CQL-Lag; offline + unconstrained fine-tuning; static best configuration; QoS-aware scheduler. Metrics: eMBB throughput, latency p99/p99.9, deadline-miss rate, cumulative violations during fine-tuning, interactions to target, inference time.
9. **Risks.**
   - The simulator's latency tail may not resemble the testbed's (sim-to-real).
   - The slicing implementation in 5G-LENA requires custom code.
   - The tail metrics need many packets.
   - Offline pre-training may give little gain if online learning is already fast.
10. **Effort.** 4–6 months for a first paper.
11. **Strength.** Medium–high (journal-level if the gain in safe sample efficiency is clear). No acceptance guarantee.

### D1: honest revision of the current paper

1. **RQ.** On real configuration logs, does a load-aware constrained policy improve on the best static configuration?
2. **Motivation.** Reviewers; Lu §X-C p. 48.
3. **Relation.** Same data.
4. **Gap.** Weak (Yang et al. 2024, CPQ).
5. **Contribution.** Audit-first methodology plus honest constrained contextual policy plus strong baselines.
6. **Changes.** Contextual-bandit formulation, state-conditional cost model, CIs.

### D4: trace-calibrated twin

1. **RQ.** Does a 5G-LENA scenario calibrated on the twinning traces rank configurations the same way as the traces do?
2. **Motivation.** Lu §XII-D p. 55: evaluate twin fidelity through action rankings and constraint violations.
3. **Relation.** It bridges the paper to D2.
4. **Gap.** Policy-level twin validation in slicing is rarely reported (to verify).
5. **Contribution.** A calibration procedure plus a fidelity metric: rank correlation of (action, load) cost and reward between traces and simulation.

**What traces without decision logs cannot support [S].** If the scheduler's per-TTI actions are not recorded, and the configuration is constant per episode, then:

- (i) No counterfactual "what if we had switched" outcomes exist.
- (ii) Transitions under switching are unidentified.
- (iii) OPE can only score policies that coincide with logged configurations ("matching", ≈ 40 % coverage), or rely on a model whose bias cannot be checked.
- (iv) Confounding of configuration with load (Paper §IV-C) biases naive estimates.

Conclusions that remain valid: per-(configuration, load) association between configuration and KPIs; the dominance of the scheduler on the latency tail *within the observed configurations*.

### 5.4 Staged architecture

- **Stage 0 (now – 1 month, existing data).** Python pipeline. P0/P1 revisions; static and oracle baselines; OSRL baselines.
- **Stage 1 (1 – 3 months, minimum credible setup for the next paper).**
  - ns-3 5G-LENA: 1 gNB, numerology μ = 1–2, 2 slices (eMBB full-buffer or video; latency-sensitive Poisson small packets), PRB quotas via RB masks or BWPs, schedulers RR/PF/QoS.
  - Per-packet latency trace from the application or FlowMonitor.
  - Python agent via ns3-ai at a 100 ms – 1 s decision period.
  - Behaviour policies with randomised switching to log decisions with propensities.
  - Traffic load driven by the twinning traces.
- **Stage 2 (3 – 6 months).** Offline → online constrained fine-tuning. Shifts in URLLC arrival rate, UE count, channel and mobility. Report cumulative violations.
- **Stage 3 (6 – 12 months, strengthens a journal paper).**
  - E2 loop via NORI or ns-O-RAN with an OSC Near-RT RIC xApp.
  - Measure loop latency.
  - Optional validation on Colosseum or srsRAN.

**Data flow (Stage 1).** ns-3 (gNB MAC, RLC buffers, application latency) → KPM aggregator (every Δ) → shared memory → Python agent (policy + shield + cost model) → action (slice PRB quota, scheduler type/weights) → ns-3 scheduler config at the next slot boundary.

**Engineering risks.**

- Run-time per seed for tail statistics: p99.9 needs ≥ 10⁴ packets per condition.
- Thread-safety of reconfiguration mid-simulation.
- 5G-LENA version drift.

### 5.5 Evaluation protocol

**Which comparison supports which claim**

| Claim | Essential comparisons |
|---|---|
| "Learning beats configuration heuristics" | Best static feasible configuration; per-load tabular oracle; QoS-aware scheduler |
| "Our safe-offline method beats existing ones" | CQL + Lagrangian (current), BCQ-Lag, CPQ, COptiDICE, CQL with fixed penalty sweep |
| "The constraint matters" | Same method with λ = 0 (ablation) |
| "Offline pre-training helps safe online learning" | Online PPO-Lag/SAC-Lag from scratch; offline-only; offline + unconstrained fine-tuning |
| "Each component matters" | Ablations: cost model (tabular vs state-conditional), shield on/off, a_{t−1} in state, history length |

**Metrics (precise definitions)**

- eMBB throughput: mean slice throughput per window (Mbps) and its 5th percentile.
- Latency: per-packet one-way latency percentiles p50/p99/p99.9 of the latency-sensitive slice, pooled per condition, with the number of packets reported.
- Deadline-miss rate: P(ℓ > L_max).
- Violation frequency: fraction of windows with c_t > d.
- Violation severity: E[(c_t − d)⁺] and CVaR₀.₉₅(c_t).
- Online stage: cumulative constraint violation Σ_t (c_t − d)⁺, and interactions to reach 95 % of the final return.
- Inference latency on the target CPU.

**Statistics**

- ≥ 10 seeds in simulation, ≥ 5 offline.
- Mean ± 95 % bootstrap CI; interquartile mean and performance profiles (Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2021).
- Paired tests across seeds for main claims.
- Report the failure rate (fraction of seeds violating d).

**Data hygiene**

- Leave-one-cluster-out *and* time-block splits.
- No transitions spanning splits.
- Hyperparameters selected on a validation cluster or on validation simulator seeds only.
- Normalisation fitted on training data.

**Conclusions allowed**

- Trace-driven experiments: association and one-step policy value under the logged support only.
- Simulation: causal and counterfactual claims, valid within the simulator's fidelity, plus sensitivity analyses on load, URLLC arrival rate, L_max, d and numerology.
